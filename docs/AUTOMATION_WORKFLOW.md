# Product Intake Automation: Workflow Design

Status: **design, not built.** Owner approval needed before each stage is implemented.
Last updated: 2026-10-14

## 1. Purpose

Find sustainable companies and products from recognised certification databases, check whether they sell on Amazon, and publish vetted products to leaflightly.com with accurate affiliate links and disclosures.

## 2. Principles

1. **Public data only.** Certification databases are read as a person would use them, through their public search. No logins, no scraping that breaks a site's terms, and no bulk downloads where the site forbids them.
2. **Amazon is read by hand, or through the API once eligible.** No automated pulls from amazon.co.uk pages. (Research found no clause allowing automated collection outside the Product Advertising API.)
3. **Nothing publishes without owner approval.** Every product passes an approval queue. The website agent never publishes on its own.
4. **Every product carries its evidence.** Each record stores the source database, the certificate or listing URL, and the date checked.
5. **Certifications apply to the product, not the company.** A brand's certification is only recorded against a product once it's confirmed for that product.
6. **Disclosure and link rules are enforced at publish time.** The Associates statement, a tracked link generated by Get Link (or the API), and the tier are all required fields.

## 3. Stages

```
[1] Certification databases  --weekly-->  [2] Company register (master file)
                                                |
                                                v
                                    [3] Amazon presence check (manual or API)
                                                |
                                                v
                         [4] Product candidate queue  (status: needs-review)
                                                |
                                  owner approves / rejects each item
                                                |
                                                v
                         [5] Amazon data capture  (owner copies link + image)
                                                |
                                                v
                         [6] Website upload  (validated, status: ready)
                                                |
                                                v
                         [7] Owner publishes  -->  live products catalogue
```

### Stage 1: Certification database watcher (weekly)

- **Input:** The database register in section 4.
- **Task:** Check each database for newly listed companies or products since the last run. Record the company name, sector, certification, certificate or listing URL, and date seen.
- **Method:** Use each database's own public search or listing pages. Where a database offers an export or RSS, use it. Where it forbids automated access in its terms, check it by hand each week.
- **Output:** `data/register/companies.json` (one record per company, with its certifications and sources).
- **Failure rule:** If a database can't be read, log it and skip. Never guess its contents.

### Stage 2: Company register (master file)

- One master file, grouped by sector and sorted alphabetically within each sector.
- A company in several sectors appears under each, with one shared record to avoid duplicates.
- Fields: company name, sectors, certifications and their sources, first seen, last checked, status (active / certificate lapsed / unknown).
- Output: `data/register/master.md` for reading, and `companies.json` for the next stage.

### Stage 3: Amazon presence check

- **Method:** A person or the research agent searches amazon.co.uk for the company name and notes whether it sells there and which products match. The agent may record **search results shown on screen**, but it doesn't open product pages in bulk.
- **Output:** For each company, a `amazon_listed: yes / no / unknown` flag, plus the candidate product names and the page they were seen on.
- **Limit:** A few checks per session, spaced out, so the activity looks like normal browsing.

### Stage 4: Candidate queue

- Each candidate product is written to `data/queue/candidates.json` with status `needs-review`.
- Required before approval: company, product name, category, proposed tier, proposed certifications (each with a source), evidence URL and date checked.
- The owner reviews the queue (a simple page or file) and sets each item to `approved` or `rejected`.

### Stage 5: Amazon data capture (owner step)

- For each approved candidate, the owner opens the product page and uses **Get Link** to generate the tracked link (short or full, as the Associates rules allow).
- The owner copies the image from Get Link where it's offered, or leaves the placeholder.
- The owner records the price as shown that day. Prices are checked again on the publish date.
- **Future:** Once the Product Advertising API is available, the price, title and image come from the API, and this stage becomes an API call. The licence limits (rate, caching, display) must be followed.

### Stage 6: Website upload

- Reads only `approved` candidates that have a tracked link, an evidence URL and a tier.
- Writes them to `src/data/products.ts` (or the database, once the catalogue moves there).
- Runs validation: required fields present, tier allowed, certifications are in the approved list, link contains the tracking ID, image is a valid URL or the placeholder.
- Sets status to `ready`. It does not deploy.

### Stage 7: Publish

- The owner reviews the `ready` items and runs the deploy.
- After deploy, the site is checked (the same smoke test as before: product pages return 200, disclosure present, links resolve).

## 6. Rules the automation must follow

- Public pages only. No logins. No credentials handled by any agent.
- No automated product-data pulls from amazon.co.uk. Owner copies links and images.
- Every product needs an evidence URL and a date checked.
- Certifications are only recorded when confirmed for that product.
- Disclosure: "As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases" appears on every page with Amazon links (already live).
- Prices are not stored as facts. They're shown only with a date, and rechecked before publishing.
- No deal or bank-offer copy unless checked on the publish day.
- Nothing publishes without owner approval.
- **Records hold our own data only.** Each database entry stores the company name, certification, date checked, and a link to the source. Do not copy a database's listing text, descriptions, product lists or images into our records.
- **Ecocert: lookups only.** One company at a time, by hand. Ecocert's robots.txt is open and its general terms don't restrict automated access, but database rights still apply to bulk extraction. No bulk pulls from the Ecocert directory without written permission.
- **Certification lookups are manual.** No bot, script or agent searches a certification database. The owner (or a person at the owner's direction) looks up each company by hand.
- **Amazon checks are manual.** The owner runs the Amazon search and generates the link with Get Link. No automated access to amazon.co.uk.
- **Affiliate programme terms are read once approved, not before.** Applications go in with the approved message. When a programme shows as Joined (or accepted), the agent reads that programme's terms page and records the key points (commission, cookie length, validation period, product feed and image rules, disclosure wording) against the programme. Terms are not read for applications still pending, so no time is spent on programmes that may reject us.

## 7. Failure handling

- A database is unreachable: log it, skip it, continue. Report at the weekly review.
- A certificate lapses or can't be found: set the company to `unknown` and remove the certification from any candidate.
- Amazon search returns no match: `amazonListed: no`. Don't add the product.
- Validation fails at Stage 6: the item stays `approved` with an error note, and nothing is written to the site.

## 10. How to run the intake (built 2026-10-14)

Files: `data/queue/candidates.json` (the queue), `scripts/intake/` (three scripts).

Each week:
1. `npm run intake:checklist` prints the Amazon search links for every candidate still marked `needs-amazon-check`.
2. Open each search by hand. If the product is listed, use Get Link, paste the link into `trackedLink`, add `imageUrl` if offered, and set `status` to `cleared`. If not listed, set `status` to `not-listed`.
3. Review `cleared` items. Set `status` to `approved` only when the certification evidence is confirmed. Add `certifications` and `certificationSources` at that point.
4. `npm run intake:validate` checks every entry. Approved items must have a tracked link with `tag=leaflightly-21`, no search-page links, and evidence.
5. `npm run intake:export` writes approved items to `data/queue/ready.ts`. Paste them into `src/data/products.ts` by hand, fill in price, rating and review count from the live page, then deploy.

Nothing in these scripts contacts Amazon or any certification database. The database watcher is not built yet, pending the terms check.

## 11. Feed catalogue (built 2026-10-16)

Feed products live in the database (`catalogue_products`, see `data/db/catalogue.sql`), not in `src/data/products.ts`. `products.ts` is still the curated list and is shown alongside approved feed products.

Each week, per feed:
1. Put the feed URL in `FEED_URL_<advertiserId>` (Vercel and `.env.local`). Never paste it into chat or commit it.
2. `node scripts/intake/feed-sync.mjs <advertiserId> [file.csv] [--dry-run]`. New items arrive unapproved. Items missing from the feed are marked removed, not deleted. The run aborts, and changes nothing, if the feed is empty or under half the live count. Every run is logged in `catalogue_sync_runs`.
3. `node scripts/intake/approve-product.mjs <advertiserId> --list` shows what's waiting. Approve each item with `--category`, `--tier` and any confirmed `--cert` values. Only approved, live items appear on the site.

Pages read through `src/lib/catalogue.ts`. If the database is unreachable, they fall back to the static list. Pages revalidate hourly.

### Variants (added 2026-10-16)
A feed often lists one product several times (colour, bristle, pack size). Each such group becomes one listed product with its options shown on the product page.
- Grouping lives in a reviewable file, e.g. `data/awin/truthbrush-groups.json`: each group name, its primary `product_id`, and each item's option label.
- Apply with `node scripts/intake/group-variants.mjs <advertiserId> <groups.json>`. Run it after each `feed-sync`. Until it runs, new rows show as separate products.
- Only the primary row of each group is listed. Options link to their own tracked URL.

### Checking feed duplicates and gaps (rule, 2026-10-16)
When a feed has products that look missing or duplicated (same name, extra variants, unlabelled options), check them on the brand's own website with a normal, non-affiliate visit (open the product page directly, not through the Awin tracking link). Do not open tracked links to check options, since each one counts as a click on the publisher's account. Record what the brand's site shows (colours, sizes, pack contents) in the grouping file, and note the source and date checked.

### Google-format feeds (decision, 2026-10-16)
Use the Awin-format feed for each advertiser. Do not load Google-format feeds for now. Their `link` field may not carry the Awin tracker, and the tracking link has not been checked. Revisit all Google-format feeds if an advertiser is only available through a Google-format feed. Cross-check the tracked link format against the Awin-format feed before loading any of them.
