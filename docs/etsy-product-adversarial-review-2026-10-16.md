# Adversarial review: Etsy product plan (16 Oct 2026)

Reviewed: the Etsy digital product plan (fifteen product concepts and the launch plan).

Method: checked the plan's stated facts against Etsy's own pages where possible, and against the plan's internal consistency. The plan's competitor figures were not re-verified in this pass.

## Facts the plan gets wrong or leaves unsupported

1. **Fees are quoted in US dollars.** The plan gives a US$0.20 listing fee and a 6.5% transaction fee. Etsy's UK sell page gives a £0.15 listing fee, the 6.5% transaction fee, and 4% + £0.20 payment processing for UK bank accounts. The UK page may be out of date, so check Etsy's current fee policy. The regulatory operating fee and the currency conversion fee also apply where relevant.
2. **The 470-page planner allocation contradicts itself.** The first table ends at page 459; the "corrected" table ends at page 470. Use one allocation. The plan also calls the planner undated but describes a 365-day sequence, which is a dated structure. Resolve this before any build.
3. **Platform claims are unsourced.** "Etsy says digital purchases cannot currently be downloaded through its app" and similar rules need a current Etsy help link before they go into FAQs.
4. **Competitor numbers can't be verified from search pages.** "Thousands of visible reviews", "Bestseller" badges and "zero sales when captured" come from personalised search results that change. The plan admits this. Don't use them as evidence of demand in listings or pitches.
5. **"Sustainable" has no defined test.** "Eco-friendly", "planet-friendly" and "chemical-free" appear across the concepts. The plan removes some of these, but the brand needs one defined standard before listings are written.

## Product-by-product challenge

| Product | Strongest argument for | Strongest argument against | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Eco-home audit | A room-by-room audit can be the entry point to the range | Free audit templates exist; the word "eco" adds nothing a buyer can check | Launch, if the output is three specific actions |
| Zero-waste challenge | Small daily actions build habit | "30 days" is common; swaps can drive new purchases | Bundle companion first |
| Pantry-first meal planner | Clear, daily problem; easy to show in an image | "Fridge-first" is already in use; plant-based focus narrows the audience | Best first product |
| Habit tracker | Quick to build; adds repeat use | Crowded and cheap; "eco days" can feel performative | Bundle bonus only |
| Budget spreadsheet | Real financial value; utility and refill costs fit the brand | Mature competition; calculations must be exact; utility bills vary | Second wave |
| Hyperlinked planner | Premium potential | Large build and testing burden; page count is a weak defence | Defer |
| Canva social kits | Real content-production need for eco businesses | Competing on template count is hard to win; prewritten claims are a risk | Separate business collection, later |
| Media kits | Helps creators present credible work | Easy to copy from cheap generic kits | Launch with the social kit, later |
| Ebook layouts | Could help small responsible publishers | Generic layouts are heavily discounted | Specialise or defer |
| Wall art | Brand aesthetic can attract gift buyers | Not an eco-living product; paper and ink are the real footprint | Optional, later |

## Where I agree with the plan

- Launch a small, specific range: meal planner, audit, decision tool and a bundle.
- Test with five real users before publishing.
- Make no measured saving or environmental improvement claims without a stated method. This matches our claims rule and the CMA Green Claims Code the plan cites.

## Where I disagree

- **Bundle first, or single products first?** The plan launches four listings together. Single products give cleaner feedback. Launch the meal planner and the decision printable first, and add the bundle once both have been tested.
- **Naming the decision tool.** "Decision dashboard" is not a term buyers search for. Use "mindful spending planner" or "buy less planner" in titles, and keep "dashboard" for a spreadsheet if one is built.
- **Spreadsheets early.** The plan puts spreadsheets in phase two. A formula-based sheet is acceptable under our rules, but it adds support load. Only build one once the printable has sold.

## Revised launch plan

**Stage 0: decide (week 1)**
- Confirm the brand promise and the single standard used for every "sustainable" word.
- Confirm UK fee and tax figures from Etsy's current policy; build the price model in GBP.
- Confirm Etsy's answer on website mentions (About page only).

**Stage 1: build the two printables (weeks 2 to 4)**
- Pantry-first meal planner: stock sheets, use-first list, seven-day plan, top-up list, one completed example. A4, US Letter and low-ink.
- Low-waste home audit: kitchen, bathroom and laundry inventories, a three-action page, a four-week follow-up, one example. A4, US Letter and low-ink.
- Gate: each product passes a user test with five people; four of five complete the main task unaided.

**Stage 2: build the decision printable (week 5)**
- One-page buy, repair, borrow or wait decision tree; comparison worksheet; two worked examples. "Unknown" is a valid answer.
- Gate: the same five-person test.

**Stage 3: packaging and QA (week 6)**
- Five files or fewer per listing, each under 20 MB; file manifest; licence; download and print guide.
- Print tests at actual size; check every link from a fresh buyer account.
- Eight listing images showing real pages and a completed example.

**Stage 4: listings (week 7)**
- Publish the meal planner and the audit first.
- Publish the decision printable once the first two have a week of observation.
- Publish the bundle once all three are live and tested.

**Stage 5: learn (weeks 8 to 12)**
- Track visits, search terms, orders, conversion, support questions, refunds and contribution per order.
- Decision rule: relevant visits with few sales means fix proof and previews; few relevant visits means fix the title and main image; repeated questions mean fix the instructions.

**Stage 6: add later, on evidence**
- Food-waste companion or budget spreadsheet: after the meal planner has sold and users ask for it.
- Repair labels (SVG) and home maintenance system: after audit users ask for repair tracking.
- Creator kits: a separate collection, once creator interest appears.
- Wall art, posters, planner: optional, one at a time, each sourced.

## Risks

- Platform rules change. Check Etsy's policy pages before each launch and each new listing.
- Support load can grow faster than sales. Track support minutes per order from the first sale.
- The brand drifts into product-review territory. Keep the shop about household decisions, not product rankings.
